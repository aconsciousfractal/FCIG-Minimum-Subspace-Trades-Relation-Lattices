\section{Reproducibility and source boundary}
\label{app:reproducibility}

The companion package is organized relative to its repository root.  The
principal fail-closed commands are
\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/check_manifest.py --closed-tree
python scripts/verify_all.py
python -O scripts/verify_all.py
python scripts/verify_manifest_only.py
\end{verbatim}
The aggregate replay invokes four independent verifiers:
\begin{verbatim}
scripts/s449c_verify_g1r_replacement_minor.py
scripts/s449c_verify_g1r_principal_determinant.py
scripts/s449f_verify_reverse_row_determinant.py
scripts/s449f_verify_rho_d1_global_gate.py
\end{verbatim}
The first and third parsers reconstruct their sparse binary matrices and
directly check the displayed diagnostic determinants.  The determinant
reconstruction verifiers audit the frozen CRT prime lists and coefficient
residues, prove signed uniqueness from row-energy bounds, recompute exact
cyclotomic norms, construct both multiplication lattices, and reproduce
the \(16\times32\) Smith form.  Generated JSON is canonical, and normal
and optimized runs are required to be byte-identical.

\paragraph{Evidence tiers.}
The package contains the two exact sparse binary submatrices, their row
and column selection manifests, the CRT inputs and outputs, and independent
arithmetic verifiers for the global determinantal-ideal gate.  The public
scripts directly recompute the two diagnostic modular determinants.  They
do not regenerate all \(189\) CRT determinants from the binaries; those
residues are source-admitted, hash-bound construction transcripts whose
congruence, bound, uniqueness, norm, and mutation consequences are audited.
The earlier census, rational-generation, local-saturation, and selected-
prime theorem blocks are distributed as compact receipts.  Their much
larger raw matrix workspace remains in the source-locked P39 archive and is
not duplicated in this reviewer-sized package.  These boundaries are
stated in the reviewer guide and claim ledger.

\paragraph{Evidence levels.}
Krotov's classification and the indispensable-fiber criterion are imported
theorems.  The local circuit proof, semisimple criteria, Bockstein
identification, Nakayama reductions, good-prime lemma, CRT uniqueness,
multiplication-lattice interpretation, and determinantal-ideal implication
are mathematical arguments in this paper.  Character tables, exhaustive
censuses, component ranks, exact minors, determinant coefficients, Smith
diagonal, and covectors are exact finite inputs with independent replays or
frozen hash-bound receipts.  Resource stops from earlier wide Smith
eliminations are not used as evidence.

\paragraph{Locked finite objects.}
The three block collections are identified by full canonical-key and
point-mask SHA--256 hashes in \texttt{docs/SOURCE\_LOCK.md}.  Their
construction route is pinned to the companion repository
\emph{FCIG--Common--Marked--Lattice--Designs} at commit
\texttt{40b3e716...d7f3227}.

\paragraph{Source boundary.}
The prior-art search is dated and bounded.  It found no source giving the
same rational-spanning, cubic-fiber, or complete primary statements at
\(137\), \(219\,097\), and \(3\,288\,036\,131\) for this locked triple,
but that negative result is not a novelty or priority certificate.  The
paper makes no priority claim and does not generalize from the three
selected configurations without a separate family argument.  General
determinantal-ideal, Smith, CRT, and cyclotomic tools are standard; no
method-priority claim is made for their use in \cref{thm:global-rho}.
