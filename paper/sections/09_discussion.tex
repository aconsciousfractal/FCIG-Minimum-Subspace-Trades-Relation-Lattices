\section{Discussion, prior art, and open directions}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The rational--integral contrast}

The minimum regulus trades generate every rational relation in all three
configurations.  Integrally, we prove saturation at
\(2,3,17,137,219\,097,3\,288\,036\,131\).  The global
determinantal-ideal certificate closes one of the six large component
gates, namely \(\rh/\cB_1\), away from \(2,3,17\).  Equality of the full
trade and relation lattices remains open because the other five large
component gates have not been computed.  Thus
\[
\text{minimum local geometry}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{full rational generation}
\ \nRightarrow\
\text{global integral saturation}.
\]
The character table also shows that common incidence parameters and a
common group do not make the relation modules isomorphic.

Cho's characteristic-zero null-design decomposition and minimum subspace
null-design work provide the complete-Grassmann background
\cite{Cho1997,Cho1998}.  Krotov supplies the minimum-support theorem and
the \(t=1\) classification \cite{Krotov2017}.  The ordinary-subset
orbit-spanning result of Ghorbani--Kamali--Khosrovshahi is the closest
structural analogue of \cref{thm:full-kernel}
\cite{GhorbaniKamaliKhosrovshahi}.  Maliakas--Stergiopoulou develop total
trades through intersection matrices and Specht modules
\cite{MaliakasStergiopoulou}.  Both ordinary-subset settings use the
symmetric-group module structure; the present theorem concerns selected
binary subspace constituents and the order-\(204\) group.

For toric ideals, Aoki--Takemura--Yoshida own the indispensable-fiber
criterion \cite{AokiTakemuraYoshida}.  Betti--Grate--Holleben--Salizzoni
develop incidence-toric/null-design language for ordinary subset
incidence matrices \cite{BettiEtAl2026}.  The present finite calculation
shows that the cubic fibers of these selected Grassmann configurations
have exactly the two regulus monomials.  Classical integral-design
modules and circuit lattices are represented by
\cite{GraverJurkat,GrahamLiLi}.  Determinantal ideals, cyclotomic
arithmetic, CRT reconstruction, and Smith normal form are standard tools
\cite{StacksProjectFitting,Cohen1993,Washington1997,Newman1972}.  These
comparisons delimit scope; no method-firstness or priority claim is made.

\subsection{A lossless rational sensor map}

The \(97\,155\) three-dimensional subspaces of \(\FF_2^8\) split into
\(515\) \(\GammaG\)-orbits, with orbit-size profile
\[
 51^5,\qquad102^{70},\qquad204^{440}.
\]
The selected three-space versus four-space incidence map
\[
 W_{3,4}:\QQ[\cB_i]\longrightarrow
 \QQ[\operatorname{Gr}_2(8,3)]
\]
has column rank \(66\,929\).  It is therefore injective on the entire
block module and, in particular, on every \(K_i\), giving a lossless
rational sensor system for the relations.

This sensing statement is not used in
\cref{thm:full-kernel,thm:theta137,thm:theta219097,%
thm:theta3288036131,thm:global-rho}; conversely, no tomography statement
is used in the integral component certificates.  Systematic comparison
of sensor families, minimal sensor sets, and reconstruction algorithms is
a separate open direction.  The calculation above records only one lossless
rational incidence sensor for the locked finite configurations.

\subsection{Remaining global integral gates}

At \(137\), \(219\,097\), and \(3\,288\,036\,131\), all six large maps
have structural rank, giving complete three-design vanishing by
\cref{thm:theta137,thm:theta219097,thm:theta3288036131}.
\Cref{thm:global-rho} goes beyond that finite list for one map.  The
remaining integral tasks are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item compute an exact localized order-maximal determinantal-ideal
  certificate for each of the other five large component maps;
  \item combine those results with \cref{prop:small-components} to prove
  or refute \(\operatorname{Supp}(\Theta_i)\subseteq\{2,3,17\}\);
  \item compute the exact global Smith invariant factors, including the
  remaining \(2\)-, \(3\)-, and \(17\)-primary structure.
\end{itemize}
A further finite-prime search cannot close these questions.  The
\(\rh/\cB_1\) result shows the required certificate shape: a common
integral cyclotomic basis, exact characteristic-zero minors of the
complete equation matrix, and their ideal sum.  The five repetitions are
deliberately deferred; none is needed for the rational, cubic,
selected-prime, or one-component global theorems proved here.

\subsection{Geometric and family questions}

Krotov's bound gives support \(30\) and leg volume \(15\) for
\(T_2(2,4,8)\), but the \(t=1\) uniqueness theorem used here does not
classify that case.  An exact next question is a two-colour census: one
leg in \(\cB_i\), the other in \(\cB_j\).  Exhaustiveness requires a
separate classification theorem.

A second direction is to replace the locked finite triple by a general
selected \(q\)-subspace configuration.  The missing theorem is nonzero
projection of the selected minimum trades to every null-design
constituent.  The component-rank criterion in
\cref{lem:component-rank} isolates what such a theorem must prove.

Finally, higher-degree parts of \(I_i\) remain open.  Cubic
indispensability neither implies cubic generation nor determines the
complete Markov or Graver basis.  The relation-lattice and sensing viewpoints
meet at
\[
 \QQ\text{-generation}\longrightarrow
 \ZZ\text{-lifting}\longrightarrow
 \text{Markov connectivity},
\]
but these stages should remain separate until their integral and
higher-degree obstructions are classified.
